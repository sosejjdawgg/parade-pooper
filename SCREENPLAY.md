**MEADOW WELL**
*A Screenplay in Four Chapters*

Animation style: Akira, Dragon Ball Z, impact scenes, beautiful smoke, amazing visuals.
Transitions: Shaun of the Dead–style snap-cut montages and whip pans between sections.

---

**ANNOTATION: PROLOGUE — “THE JOYRIDE”**
*Pulp Fiction opens mid-conversation and mid-crime. We drop the audience into the driver’s seat with no backstory, no names, no moral framing. The two youths exist only as motion and adrenaline. The camera never leaves their perspective until the crash. Speed, sound, and light do the exposition. We cut on the impact the way Tarantino cuts on a gunshot — hard, final, and before the audience is ready.*

**FADE IN:**

**EXT. COAST ROAD – NIGHT**

A stolen Renault. Rain on the windscreen. Headlights carving white tunnels through the dark.

THE DRIVER (17) grips the wheel with one hand, cigarette between his teeth. THE PASSENGER (21) leans out the window, laughing into the slipstream, hair whipping.

The speedometer climbs past 100. 110. 120.

A police car appears in the rear-view. Distant. Steady. Not gaining. Not falling back.

**DRIVER**
They’re still there.

**PASSENGER**
They’re always there. Floor it.

The engine screams. The car hits 125. The world outside blurs into streaks of sodium light and wet tarmac.

They take a bend too hard. The rear steps out. The Driver corrects — almost. The lamppost rises out of the dark like a concrete fist.

**IMPACT.**

Metal folds. Glass detonates. The car spins, rolls once, and comes to rest on its roof. Flames crawl across the spilled fuel. Inside, nothing moves.

The police car pulls up half a mile back. Lights flashing. Too late.

**CUT TO BLACK.**

---

**ANNOTATION: CHAPTER ONE — “THREE DAYS LATER”**
*Tarantino loves the sudden time jump after violence. We leave the crash and arrive in the middle of the consequence. No funeral, no investigation montage. Just the estate already boiling. The two dead youths are now graffiti and rumour — present but absent, the way Vincent Vega is present after he’s already been shot. Action is the language. Dialogue is short and functional.*

**EXT. MEADOW WELL ESTATE – NIGHT (THREE DAYS LATER)**

Darkness. An electricity substation burns. The whole estate is blacked out except for the orange glow of half a dozen fires.

Youths in hoods and tracksuits move like a single organism. Bricks in hands. Petrol bombs ready.

A fish-and-chip shop is already gutted. Asian shopkeepers are dragged from a flat above a newsagent. Someone shouts:

**VOICE**
Burn ’em out!

The building goes up. Smoke rolls across the street.

**EXT. AVON AVENUE – CONTINUOUS**

A makeshift barricade of shopping trolleys and burning cars blocks the road. Police vans sit at the edge of the estate, lights spinning, not advancing.

A brick hits a windscreen. Then another. Firefighters try to get a hose on the substation. A shower of missiles drives them back.

**YOUTH 1**
This is for them!

**YOUTH 2**
They forced them off the road!

No one corrects him. The rumour is already fact.

A stolen car does donuts in the middle of the junction, tyres screaming, headlights cutting through the smoke like searchlights. Four hundred people are on the streets. Most watch. Some throw. All feel it.

**CUT TO:**

**EXT. WEST END, NEWCASTLE – NIGHT (LATER)**

The Dodds Arms pub. A petrol bomb arcs through the night and detonates inside. The roof collapses in a roar of flame. Three hundred youths on the roofs of terraces throw tiles at the police vans below. The high street is already in ruins.

**CUT TO BLACK.**

---

**ANNOTATION: CHAPTER TWO — “THE BILL”**
*Pulp Fiction’s “The Bonnie Situation” is about the cost of the mess. Here the mess has a price tag and a political response. We stay visual and kinetic — no talking heads. Regeneration is shown as physical change: demolition, new brick, cameras. The tone stays cold and observational, the way Tarantino watches a character clean up blood.*

**EXT. MEADOW WELL – DAY (MONTHS LATER)**

Bulldozers. 750 houses come down. New ones go up in their place. A community centre rises where the youth club burned. CCTV cameras watch every corner.

A police station opens. Later it will close.

On a wall that survived, faded graffiti still reads:
**DALE & COLIN – NOT THE SCORE**

Residents walk past it without looking.

**INT. NEW COMMUNITY CENTRE – DAY**

Kids play football. A worker locks up. Outside, a burnt-out car sits on the grass verge like a monument that never got removed.

**CUT TO BLACK.**

---

**ANNOTATION: EPILOGUE — “BONFIRE NIGHT”**
*Tarantino’s endings often loop back to the beginning with a different angle. We return to fire, speed, and rumour. The same estate. Different spark. Same energy. The two youths from the opening are long gone, but the machine they left behind is still running. We end mid-action, the way the film begins.*

**EXT. WATERVILLE ROAD, MEADOW WELL – NIGHT (5 NOVEMBER 2026)**

Fireworks. Not the celebratory kind.

A building under construction. A banner on the wall:
**OUR PROMISE TO THE COMMUNITY OF MEADOWELL
NO ASYLUM SEEKERS WILL BE HOUSED HERE**

A car reverses at speed into the front wall. Metal screams. The car is set alight. Flames climb the brickwork.

Riot police form a line. Bricks and rockets hit the shields. Three fire engines are already damaged. Officers are down.

**VOICE (O.S.)**
It’s an HMO! They’re putting them in there!

It isn’t. It’s a supermarket with flats above. The truth doesn’t matter. The fire does.

A litter picker in a high-vis jacket stands on the edge of the chaos, phone out, recording. Around him, residents start picking up bricks before the sun comes up.

The estate burns in small, precise places.

**FADE OUT.**

**THE END**
